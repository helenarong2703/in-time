In Time / 时差

Re-timing the Existing City · 重校既有城市的时间 — 43 projects across six temporal dimensions

Curatorial statement

Every act of urban renewal sets a clock. A demolition order, a lease, a handover date, a monitoring interval: each decides how long a building may stand, how long a resident must wait, how often a system looks again, and when a place is declared finished. Materials, ecologies, lives, labor, budgets, and computation all run at their own rates, and renewal forces them into a single schedule. The differences that result are not abstract. They decide who can remain, who must wait, what receives care, what becomes urgent, and what becomes obsolete. In Time / 时差 asks who sets the clock of urban change — and what architecture and artificial intelligence can re-time.

“万物并作，吾以观复。” Read as an invitation to watch things emerging together and to know them by their returns, the line from the *Tao Te Ching* introduces the city as a convergence of processes that recur: seasons, repairs, leases, generations, updates. 复 here is not decorative cyclicality. It is recurrence and consequence — what comes back after a project is declared complete, and what must be maintained for anything to last.

Situated in Chinese urban renewal, the exhibition treats architecture and AI as temporal operators. Architecture redistributes duration through space: it decides what endures, what is interrupted, and what can adapt — extending a structure’s useful life, slowing a disruptive change, accelerating an urgent repair, holding open a recovery. AI redistributes attention and urgency: by determining what is sensed and how often, how far ahead a model predicts, at what threshold a deviation triggers action, and when a model is updated or retired, computational systems help set the pace of transformation. Their apparent speed rests on slower infrastructures of energy, material, and labor, and their predictions meet consequences that arrive later — through weathering, ecological response, and human use. Both can widen the temporal possibilities of a place. Both can also compress its many times into one metric, one schedule, one forecast. The question is how that power is governed.

Each contribution takes the form of a time section. As an architectural section reveals relations across space, a time section aligns the processes that constitute a place with those that organize its change. Six timelines form its legend — Material, Ecological, Cultural, Maintenance, Financial, Technological — three that make a place and three that remake it. Every case begins from one hinge event, foregrounds the two to four timelines that explain it, and answers the same four questions: what changed, who waited, who maintained it, and what remains unresolved. The catalogue files each project under the timeline it was commissioned to serve; the timeline it fails to keep pace with is its 时差.

The pool reveals its own finding. Architects are commissioned to deliver material and cultural outcomes; almost no one is commissioned to maintain or to fund a place past handover. Maintenance and Financial are the thinnest categories in Chinese renewal not because they matter least but because they are rarely made visible. The exhibition makes them visible — through commissioned afterlife audits, workers’ and residents’ records, and an art layer that keeps time changing across the six months of the pavilion.

Together the cases present the existing city as unfinished: already made, still changing, and available for careful re-timing — 校时 — so that what remains useful can remain, what needs care receives it, what demands change is changed in time, and the power to decide is held in common.

策展陈述（草稿）

每一次城市更新都在设定一台时钟。一纸拆除令、一份租约、一个交付日期、一个监测周期，分别决定着一栋建筑还能立多久、一位居民还要等多久、一个系统多久回看一次、一个地方何时被宣布"完成"。材料、生态、生命、劳动、资金与计算各自以不同的速率运行，而更新迫使它们进入同一张日程表。由此产生的差异并不抽象：它们决定谁能留下、谁必须等待、什么得到照护、什么变得紧迫、什么被宣告过时。《在时之中 / 时差》追问：谁在设定城市变化的时钟？建筑与人工智能又能重校什么？

"万物并作，吾以观复。"若把这句《道德经》读作一种邀请——去观看万物并起，并从其往复中认识它们——那么城市便呈现为诸多循环过程的汇聚：季节、修缮、租期、世代、更新。这里的"复"不是装饰性的循环意象，而是复归与后果：项目被宣告完成之后仍会回来的东西，以及任何事物若要长久便必须持续维护的部分。

展览立足中国城市更新，把建筑与人工智能视为时间的操作者。建筑通过空间重新分配时长：它决定什么得以延续、什么被打断、什么能够调适——延长结构的使用寿命、放缓破坏性的变化、加快紧迫的修缮、为恢复留出时间。人工智能重新分配注意力与紧迫感：通过决定感知什么、多久感知一次、模型预测多远、偏差达到何种阈值触发行动、模型何时更新或退役，计算系统参与设定着城市转型的节奏。它们表面的速度依托于能源、材料与劳动等更慢的基础设施，它们的预测也会遭遇之后才显现的后果——风化、生态回应与人的使用。二者都能拓宽一个地方的时间可能性，也都可能把它的多重时间压缩为一个指标、一张日程、一次预测。问题在于这种权力如何被治理。

每一件参展作品都以时间剖面的形式呈现。正如建筑剖面揭示空间中的关系，时间剖面把构成一个地方的过程与组织其变化的过程对齐。六条时间线构成其图例——材料、生态、文化、维护、资金、技术——三条造就地方，三条重塑地方。每个案例从一个枢纽事件出发，突出最能解释它的两到四条时间线，并回答同样四个问题：什么改变了？谁在等待？谁在维护？什么悬而未决？图录按每个项目受委托服务的那条时间线归档；它未能跟上的那条时间线，即是它的时差。

这个项目池揭示了它自己的发现：建筑师受托交付材料与文化的成果，却几乎无人受托在交付之后去维护、去持续出资。维护与资金是中国城市更新中最薄弱的两类，并非因为它们最不重要，而是因为它们最少被看见。展览让它们被看见——通过委托的"后续使用审计"、工人与居民的记录，以及一个在展馆六个月中持续让时间发生变化的艺术层。

这些案例共同呈现一座未完成的既有城市：已然建成，仍在变化，并可被审慎地重校——校时——让仍然有用的得以留存，让需要照护的得到照护，让必须改变的及时改变，并让决定的权力被共同持有。

How to read each card. Every project holds all six temporal dimensions — 材料 Material · 生态 Ecological · 文化 Cultural · 维护 Maintenance · 资金 Financial · 技术 Technological — in different proportions. The hexagonal time-section profile shows them bulging to different degrees (3 = the dimension the project was commissioned to serve, its filing category; 2 = dimensions it foregrounds; 1 = present but latent). “Out of time with” names the dimension it fails to keep pace with: that gap is the project's 时差. Cards marked Curatorial call are my selection judgments for Helena to keep or flip; Gate lines are the verification conditions before any invitation.
